# Next — market check (honest)

**Question:** Is there a market, or wrong tree?  
**Team view:** Pain is real. Category takes money. Your *exact* product is not proven yet.  
**Verdict:** **Not the wrong tree — but not a free lunch.** Treat it as a **thin / unproven wedge** until interviews say otherwise.

---

## Plain English answer

**People hate admin and “what should I do now?”** That’s real.

**People already pay $15–40/month** for tools that plan the day or protect the calendar (Motion, Sunsama, Reclaim, Akiflow, etc.). So money exists.

**The risk:** Your big vision (read everything, one Next, near-zero admin) sounds like *every* AI productivity pitch. Crowded. Easy to become “nice idea.”

So: **don’t quit.** Also **don’t assume** the market will buy *your* version until strangers say they’d switch and pay.

---

## Evidence the pain is real

- CS / sales folks often lose ~**40%** of time to admin / reactive junk (industry writeups).
- QBR / prep alone can eat many hours a week for CSMs.
- AI task / productivity tools are a growing category; companies keep buying “save time” software.
- Separate email AI + calendar AI + Slack AI still don’t act as **one** work brain — that gap matches your vision.

## Evidence you could be in a hard fight

- Motion / Sunsama / Reclaim / Akiflow / Todoist AI / ChatGPT already own “help me plan my day.”
- Solo buyers have small budgets; they only pay if you **replace** something or feel 10x better daily.
- “Plugs into everything” is hard (trust, privacy, IT). Enterprise is slower and scarier.
- A pretty demo ≠ people change their morning habit.

---

## Who might actually pay

Not “everyone.”

Best early buyers:
- Skilled ICs / managers in **CS, sales, SE, tech** whose day is interrupts + prep  
- Or a **founder / lead** who feels dropped balls and will put it on a card  

They buy **time back** and **fewer misses** — not another list.

---

## What your interviews must prove (~10 talks)

Ask until you know:

1. Do they feel this pain **every day** (not once a month)?  
2. What do they use now — and would they **drop or stop paying** something for Next?  
3. Would they let a tool read calendar/email (trust)?  
4. Would they pay **about $X/month** — get a number?  
5. Is the magic “**one Next**” — or do they just want better notes/scheduling?

**Good signs:** “I’d switch.” Names a tool to replace. Says a monthly number without flinching. Asks when they can try it.

**Bad signs:** “Cool.” “I’d use free ChatGPT.” “I need my whole company IT to approve first” (for v1). No tool they’d drop. Won’t share inbox/calendar ever.

---

## Kill criteria (stop or hard pivot)

After ~10 solid interviews, **pause or pivot** if:
- Nobody names a tool they’d replace  
- “Nice to have” under ~$15–20 and no urgency  
- They won’t grant the inputs that make Next real  
- They describe it as “another to-do / calendar app” and don’t care about one-Next execution  

Finance kill later (after a product exists): people don’t come back (weak month-2 retention).

---

## What this means for you this week

1. Do the interviews (you planned that — good).  
2. Use the questions above — write answers down.  
3. Don’t build the full “everything connected” product yet.  
4. Look for **one role** that feels the pain hardest (even if brand stays “anyone”).
